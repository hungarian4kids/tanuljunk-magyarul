🏫 Iskola

Getting ready for a Hungarian school? Here's the vocabulary, phrases, and everyday know-how that will help you feel at home from day one.

💡 The ellenőrző is a small booklet every Hungarian student carries — teachers write grades, notes, and messages to parents in it. There isn't really a US equivalent, so it's worth asking about on the first day!

These full sentences will help far more than single words — tap the speaker to hear each one.

🤔 Segítségkérés (Asking for help)

Nem értem.
I don't understand.
Elmondanád még egyszer, kérlek?
Could you say that again, please?
Hogy mondják ezt magyarul?
How do you say this in Hungarian?
Leírnád, kérlek?
Could you write it down, please?

🙋 Alapvető szükségletek (Basic needs)

Kimehetek a mosdóba?
Can I go to the bathroom?
Ihatok egy kis vizet?
Can I have some water?
Rosszul érzem magam.
I don't feel well.
Elfelejtettem a füzetemet.
I forgot my notebook.

🤝 Barátkozás (Making friends)

Szia, hogy hívnak?
Hi, what's your name?
Játszhatok veletek?
Can I play with you?
Ez az enyém.
This is mine.
Szeretnél a barátom lenni?
Would you like to be my friend?

✋ Óra alatt (During class)

Jelentkezhetek?
May I raise my hand?
Nem tudom a választ.
I don't know the answer.
Befejeztem.
I'm finished.
Kaphatok még egy percet?
Can I have one more minute?

🙇 Hogyan szólítsd a tanítót/tanárt

Younger grades usually have one main teacher, called tanító néni (female) or tanító bácsi (male). Older grades have subject teachers: tanár néni / tanár úr.

Hungarian kids often speak to adults using a polite "tetszik" construction instead of just asking directly — e.g. instead of "Kimehetek?", you'll often hear "Kimehetek, tanító néni?" or the softer "Szabad kimennem?" It's not required to master this right away, but it's good to recognize.

🔔 Csengetés és szünet

A bell (csengő) rings at the start and end of every class period (óra). Between classes there's a short break (szünet) — usually 10 minutes, with one longer break for lunch.

🔢 Osztályzás

Hungarian schools grade on a 1–5 scale, where 5 (jeles) is the best grade and 1 (elégtelen) means failing — the opposite direction from an A–F scale!

📓 Házi feladat és ellenőrző

Homework is házi feladat, often written on the board at the end of class. Grades and teacher notes go into the ellenőrző (report booklet) — it's important to bring it every day and show it to a parent.

🍽️ Menza

School lunch is called menza. Many schools also offer a mid-morning snack break. If your child has food preferences or allergies, it's worth mentioning to the teacher early on.

✋ Jelentkezés

To answer or ask a question, students raise a hand — this is called jelentkezés. Calling out without raising a hand is generally discouraged, a bit more formally than in many US classrooms.
